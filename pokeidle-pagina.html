Left off the war roster? Now the reserves have a fight of their own: every reserve in the guild teams up against an extremely strong Pokemon from... OUTLAND!

In the game: Guild → Boss Raid, next to War.

How it works

  • Automatic: if the owner signed the guild up for the Guild War, every reserve with a team fights on their own, at war time, with their saved war team (or their hunt team). No need to be online.
  • One boss per guild: each guild faces a different Outland Pokemon, with the Gigantamax Raid mechanics and moves in its type — one area move, one on the target and the roar that sweeps the arena.
  • HP: 25,000 per reserve in the fight. A guild with more reserves faces a bigger boss.
  • Prize: up to +5% XP and farm for the whole guild until the next war, by the HP left on the boss (30% left → +3.5%). The full 5% only by taking it down. The roster adds it to the ranking bonus: TOP 3 with the boss down gets +8% and +5%.

The Boss Raid tab

  • Shows the next boss a day ahead — its moves and the types it is weak to, resists or is immune to —, how much HP it will have and the list of reserves who will fight. Get your team ready with it in mind.
  • The last fight stays there, with the replay — only your guild’s. When the fight ends, everyone in the guild who is online gets a notice.

On the Trainer Card

  • New Guild Boss line, in red: the boss bonus and the time until the next war. Click it to open the Boss Raid tab.
  • Boosts got some color: VIP in shiny blue, each boost in the color of its icon (XP Boost Pokemon in rainbow) and the guild ranking bonus in the Guild Boost purple.
  • Every card takes you somewhere: click a boost to open the Shop on its type, VIP for the VIP section and Guild for PvP → Guild.

PokeIdle’s rules allow up to 4 accounts per person. Now you show the team which ones are yours.

How to link

  • Log in to the game with one of your accounts and open pokeidle.io/vincular.
  • Type the e-mail of the other account: a 6-digit code arrives at that e-mail and is valid for 15 minutes.
  • Type the code on the page and that is it: the two accounts are linked. Any account in the group can link the next one, up to 4 accounts.

PvP now happens on a board: you place up to 5 pokemon on the squares of your half of the arena, and they start together and fight at the same time. Whoever stands in front takes the hits; whoever stands in the back attacks from afar.

Set up your board

  • Drag and drop: in PvP › Ranked, tap Edit board and drag pokemon from the Team, the Collection or the Depot onto the squares (on a phone, tap the pokemon and then the square). Dropping on another one swaps them, and Auto-place sorts everything out: melee in front, ranged in the back.
  • Position decides: on the board, every pokemon’s HP counts ×1.5, so fights last longer. The trainer stays put at the entrance.
  • Secret squares: nobody sees where you put each one, not on your profile, not on the Tracker; your opponent only finds out in the fight. Saved formations keep the squares.

Each pokemon’s range

  • 🗡️ Melee attacks the next square. N🏹 attacks from afar, up to N squares (2🏹, 3🏹 or 4🏹), in any direction, diagonals included.
  • It belongs to the species: it comes from the base stats, by SP.ATK − the higher of ATK and HP. High ATK or HP fights up close; high SP.ATK with little HP attacks from afar, and the bigger the gap, the farther.
  • Same for the whole species: IV, quality, potency, refinement, level, shiny and legendary don’t touch the range. The badge shows on the pokemon’s sheet and on every PvP screen.

War placing is now a score from 0 to 100: 60 for staying alive — the last guild standing gets all 60 — and 40 for fighting: how many enemy Pokemon the guild knocked out. Lasting still counts the most, but hiding alone no longer takes anyone to the top.

How the war scores

  • Survival — up to 60: 60 ÷ √ of the position the guild fell in, counting from the last one standing. The last one gets 60; 2nd, 42.4; 3rd, 34.6; 10th, 19; 20th, 13.4. The weight sits at the top: outlasting the runner-up is worth a lot, and in the middle of the table fighting decides.
  • Combat — up to 40: a counter of enemy Pokemon knocked out by the guild — any member's knockout counts. Same math: 40 ÷ √ of the guild's position on that counter. The one with the most knockouts gets 40; 2nd, 28.3; 3rd, 23.1; 10th, 12.6. No knockouts gets 0, and guilds tied on knockouts share the position.
  • The score is the sum of both and decides the placing; on a tie, whoever fell last wins. The GP a guild earns is its score, rounded — .5 or less rounds down, above .5 rounds up: a 65.5 score gives +65 GP, 40.8 gives +41. The guild ranking bonus and the Global Season are unchanged.

THE POKEMON FEE WENT DOWN

13%up to 500 gems
10%from 501 to 5,000
7.5%above 5,000

Selling a pokemon for gems always cost 15%. Now the fee comes in brackets: the bigger the sale, the lower the rate — and the big sale, the one that used to leave the game, finally fits in here.

How the brackets work

  • The fee is marginal, like income tax: each bracket applies only to its own slice. A 10,000 sale pays 13% on the first 500, 10% on the next slice and 7.5% only on what goes past 5,000 — an effective rate of 9.0%, not 7.5% on everything.
  • That is why asking 1 gem more never pays less. With flat brackets there would be a cliff at 501 and another at 5,001, and the whole market would huddle right below them. Here a higher price always pays better.
  • It applies to POKEMON, which are one of a kind: the whole listing is the sale. Items and diamonds stay at a flat 15% — those are bought in bulk, and a buyer could slice the purchase just to pin the seller in the top bracket.

And what went up: Coins, from 10% to 15%

  • Sending Coins to a friend has always burned 15%. While the Market charged 10%, it was the cheapest way to move gold — listing anything for 1 million and having a friend buy it cost 100k, against 150k through the front door.
  • Both numbers are now the same, and choosing between the two is back to being about convenience. Gold is minted on every kill and suffers from inflation — there the fee is a sink, and it is what holds down the price of everything you buy.

A Boss sheet now tells you how to catch it

  • Every Boss sheet traded the "COMING SOON" tag for a Mystic Ticket panel: where the ticket drops, the chance of drawing that exact legendary, its level in the arena and the catch table per ball.
  • It covers the 25 Bosses with an arena and the event ones — and Victini, which by an oversight could never come out of a ticket, joined the draw.

Move cooldowns now belong to the pokemon

  • A cooldown only runs while the pokemon is on the field. Switching pauses what is left and keeps it with that pokemon; back on the field, it picks up where it stopped. Switching for type advantage neither costs nor earns a tenth of a second.
  • It used to be possible to fire the signature move of five pokemon in a row and find the first one already recharged — ~40% more damage on a hunt, and easy to automate. A player reported it on Discord: thank you. Healing at the Center also stopped clearing cooldowns; only the paid arenas (Boss and Mystic) reset them, which is what they always promised.
